# LOCAL MARKET RESEARCH — EASTERN WAKE COUNTY

## Target Communities
- **Knightdale (ZIP 27545):** Population ~19,000+. Rapidly expanding with master-planned subdivisions (Knightdale Station, Glenmere, Widewaters) and established communities (Planters Walk). Mix of sun-tolerant Bermuda grass and shaded Tall Fescue lawns.
- **Wendell (ZIP 27591):** Population ~11,000+. Major growth driven by Wendell Falls (huge master-planned community with strict HOA landscaping guidelines), Edgemont Landing, and surrounding parcels.
- **Zebulon (ZIP 27597):** Population ~8,000+. Known as "The Town of Friendly People." Features older estate homesites, Weaver's Pond, Autumn Lakes, and large rolling yards with heavy tree cover.

## Homeowner Pain Points in Eastern Wake
1. **Contractor Unresponsiveness:** Local solo operators often do not return calls or emails during peak spring/summer months.
2. **Scheduling Inconsistency:** Providers showing up irregularly or skipping weeks without notice.
3. **Property Gate/Pet Issues:** Contractors leaving gates unlatched or blowing clippings into pools and flower beds.
4. **Heavy Clay Soil:** Piedmont red clay compacts easily, leading to drainage issues and thin grass if not properly aerated and mowed at appropriate heights.

## Local Grass Varieties & Maintenance Windows
- **Tall Fescue:** Cool-season turf. Best mowed at 3.5–4 inches. Aerated and overseeded September–October.
- **Bermuda & Zoysia:** Warm-season turf. Best mowed at 1.5–2.5 inches. Active mowing April–October. Goes dormant/brown in winter.
- **Pine Straw vs. Mulch:** Longleaf pine straw is regionally preferred for sloped beds and pine tree understories; double-shredded hardwood mulch is preferred for formal front foundation beds.
